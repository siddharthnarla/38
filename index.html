What the tiers mean

Counties are classified by the highest level of IBD care verifiably available, based on hospital IBD program pages, physician bios, specialty directories, and Crohn's & Colitis Foundation listings.

Tier 1Formal IBD Center
Criteria: A named, multidisciplinary IBD program — gastroenterologists who subspecialize in Crohn's and ulcerative colitis working alongside colorectal surgery, nutrition, advanced imaging, and typically clinical trials.
For a patient: Complex or treatment-resistant disease can be managed without leaving the region. Access to newer therapies and trials.
In Washington: King County only — UW Medicine IBD Center, Virginia Mason, Swedish, and Seattle Children's (pediatric).
Tier 2Fellowship-trained / research-active specialist
Criteria: At least one gastroenterologist with formal IBD subspecialty training (such as a Crohn's & Colitis Foundation IBD fellowship) or an active IBD publication record — but no formal multidisciplinary center.
For a patient: Expert-level IBD management is available locally. Coordination across surgery, nutrition, and imaging may require outside referral.
Clark, Skagit, Whatcom.
Tier 3IBD-focused providers with on-site infusion
Criteria: Gastroenterologists who identify IBD as a clinical focus, and the practice administers biologic infusion therapy on site.
For a patient: The practical difference is significant — patients on infused biologics receive treatment locally instead of traveling for every dose, often every 6–8 weeks.
Spokane, Benton.
Tier 4Named IBD-focused provider
Criteria: At least one gastroenterologist whose professional profile names IBD, Crohn's disease, or ulcerative colitis as a clinical focus. No confirmed subspecialty fellowship or on-site infusion.
For a patient: You can see a physician who concentrates on IBD, but advanced or infused therapy may still require travel.
Snohomish, Pierce (pediatric), Chelan, Yakima, Kitsap, Cowlitz, Walla Walla.
Tier 5General gastroenterologist
Criteria: Gastroenterology care exists in the county, and those physicians treat IBD as part of a general practice spanning the full range of digestive disease. No provider identifies IBD as a focus.
For a patient: Routine monitoring and management are possible locally. Complex, refractory, or rapidly changing disease will likely require referral out of county.
Thurston, Franklin, Whitman, Asotin, Okanogan, Adams, Mason, Lewis, Grant, Clallam, Grays Harbor, Kittitas, Stevens, San Juan, Island.
Tier 6Medical desert
Criteria: No gastroenterologist of any kind practices in the county. In some counties, endoscopy is performed by general surgeons rather than gastroenterologists.
For a patient: All gastroenterology and IBD care requires leaving the county — for every appointment, scope, and infusion.
11 counties: Douglas, Jefferson, Klickitat, Pacific, Pend Oreille, Skamania, Lincoln, Ferry, Wahkiakum, Columbia, Garfield — 184,456 residents.
Note on classification: Tiers reflect verifiable public evidence of IBD-focused care, not the quality of any individual physician. A general gastroenterologist may manage IBD expertly; the tier records what a patient can confirm before choosing where to seek care. Absence of a public IBD focus is recorded as absence of evidence, not evidence of absence.
